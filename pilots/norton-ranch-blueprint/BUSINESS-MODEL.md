# M5 Sovereign Agriculture — Business Model

## The proposition

## Stewardship economy

The economic model should reward care rather than extraction.

M5 can support businesses that help people care for land, animals, crops, soil, water, forests, habitat and shared natural infrastructure while keeping the underlying knowledge and operating history with the lawful steward. The value of the business is the service it provides — hardware, installation, analysis, restoration, veterinary or agronomic expertise, maintenance, provenance and continuity — not permanent capture of the farmer's or community's data.

The same design principle can extend from farms to watersheds, oceans, lakes, wetlands, forests, trails and other places where human systems have responsibilities that reach beyond ownership.

A farm or ranch should be able to buy hardware without permanently renting access to the data produced by its own animals and operations.

M5 Sovereign Agriculture separates the market into four layers:

### 1. Public commons

The Foundation publishes standards, schemas, reference implementations, source/license registries, model methods, and conformance tests.

### 2. Farmer-owned private intelligence

Raw sensor data, operational history, individual-animal baselines, derived herd intelligence, business records, and private evidence remain controlled by the farmer or lawful farm/ranch entity.

### 3. Competitive hardware

A farmer can use conforming collars, tags, gateways, cameras, environmental sensors, and local compute from multiple manufacturers.

### 4. Competitive services

Independent businesses can earn revenue from hardware, installation, managed local infrastructure, pasture analytics, breeding support, veterinary integration, data migration, training, model tuning, and other services without owning the farmer's underlying data.

## Proposed M5 annual activation

The public standard remains price-neutral.

For the M5 implementation, the proposed base is:

**M5 Farm/Business Entity — $840 per year**

The implementation can include:

- business/entity registry context;
- M5 namespace;
- jurisdiction mapping;
- accountable-human and signer relationships;
- business M5POD context;
- farm/location registry;
- herd registry;
- animal registry tools;
- device registry and reassignment;
- local import/export utilities;
- software updates for the M5 implementation;
- conformance manifests and receipts.

The base implementation does **not** impose a required per-animal M5 software charge.

Government incorporation/registration charges, livestock program fees, third-party hardware, installation, cellular/satellite service, support contracts, specialist analytics, veterinary services, insurance, and other external costs remain separate.

## OEM / private-label opportunity

A collar or livestock-technology business can sell a preconfigured package:

```text
HARDWARE MANUFACTURER / PRIVATE LABEL
        ↓
MODEL + FIRMWARE + BATCH
        ↓
SERIALIZED COLLARS / SENSORS
        ↓
GATEWAY + LOCAL COMPUTE
        ↓
SHIPMENT MANIFEST
        ↓
FARM CLAIMS SHIPMENT
        ↓
BULK HERD IMPORT
        ↓
DEVICE ↔ ANIMAL ASSIGNMENT
        ↓
LOCAL OPERATION
```

A 500-head package could arrive with:
- 500 serialized collars;
- one or more local gateways;
- optional local compute;
- a signed device manifest;
- a bulk provisioning file;
- firmware/SBOM evidence;
- warranty/service terms;
- M5-compatible assignment records.

The farmer claims the shipment under the farm entity, imports or creates the herd manifest, and assigns devices without manually building hundreds of records.

## Data-sharing market

Farmers may later choose to license or contribute selected, de-identified, purpose-limited data to research, cooperatives, universities, insurers, breeders, veterinarians, buyers, or a public commons.

The architecture must treat this as an affirmative transaction with:
- defined data scope;
- purpose;
- recipient;
- duration;
- restrictions;
- compensation where applicable;
- revocation/expiry where applicable;
- receipts.

Silence or device operation is not blanket consent to secondary model training.

## Value proposition

**Register the principal. Register the business. Claim the hardware. Register the herd. Run locally. Export when you choose. Change vendors without losing the history.**
