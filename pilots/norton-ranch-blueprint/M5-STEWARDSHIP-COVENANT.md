# M5 Stewardship Covenant

**Draft for public review — October 3, 2026**

We are caretakers before we are owners.

Human beings create titles, contracts, registries, markets and technologies so that we can organize responsibility. Those tools matter, but they do not make the Earth a machine for extraction, nor do they turn living beings into mere rows in a database.

Our responsibility reaches across the places and lives entrusted to our care: land and soil, plants and animals, farms and forests, oceans and rivers, lakes and wetlands, wildlife habitat, trails and the shared spaces that connect communities.

M5 systems should help us know who is responsible, preserve what happened, carry knowledge forward and make care visible without making private life or the natural world another surveillance commodity.

We distinguish title from stewardship, authority from responsibility, data from life, and economic value from inherent worth.

Where a human being or lawful entity has authority to act, that authority should be exercised with care for the people, living systems and sentient beings affected by it.

Where there is no private owner — an ocean, watershed, habitat, trail or other commons — the absence of ownership does not mean the absence of responsibility.

The public commons exists so that communities, farmers, builders, researchers and future stewards do not have to begin again each generation. Knowledge that can safely be shared should strengthen the commons. Private operational knowledge should remain with the people and communities responsible for it unless they affirmatively choose to share it.

The aim is not to place nature inside a ledger.

The aim is to build human systems worthy of what we have been given to protect.
