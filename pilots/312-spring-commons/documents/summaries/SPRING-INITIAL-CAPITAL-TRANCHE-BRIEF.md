## The opportunity

312 Spring Commons is a proposed public-benefit rehabilitation and operating
project centered on the historic federal courthouse at 312 N. Spring Street. The
building is on GSA's accelerated-disposition list and current research identifies
a potential Historic Monument Public Benefit Conveyance pathway through an
eligible governmental grantee.

The property is approximately **751,791 rentable square feet**. GSA has also
cited approximately **35% vacancy** and nearly **$153M of capital-maintenance
liabilities** associated with disposition.

## Working capital architecture

The broader project is being modeled as a **$200M+ blended-capital
rehabilitation and activation program**, potentially combining public and
philanthropic capital, PRIs, Historic Tax Credits, New Markets Tax Credits,
project debt/equity, tenant/operator capital, and reserves.

Final capital requirements remain subject to engineering, title, environmental,
tax, legal, and government diligence.

## Initial tranche

**Illustrative target: $25M**

Purpose: establish an initial professionally structured pool of project capital
capable of supporting approved early-phase rehabilitation/activation
requirements and demonstrating financeability of the larger capital stack.

Potential uses, subject to definitive diligence and documentation:

- predevelopment and project mobilization;
- architecture, engineering, and preservation work;
- capital-condition and environmental diligence;
- initial rehabilitation, life-safety, and systems work;
- transaction, escrow, and financing costs;
- approved operating/reserve requirements;
- eligible project infrastructure.

**This tranche is not assumed to purchase the federal real estate.** Under the
preferred public-benefit pathway, the underlying title could remain public while
separately structured capital finances rehabilitation and defined project
economics. The feasibility research identifies rehabilitation and operating
obligations--not necessarily a market-price land acquisition--as the primary
financing challenge.

## What makes the project different

The transaction architecture is being developed in a public Commons with open
data standards and an onchain-native framework to support traditional and
digital asset finance:

```text
title
-> public authority
-> capital
-> escrow
-> project rights
-> credentials
-> human authorization
-> bounded automation
-> evidence
```

AI assists with research, reconciliation, and workflow. **AI does not create
legal authority. Humans and accountable institutions authorize consequential
actions.**

The Commons includes draft deal documents and public review materials covering
capital, escrow, leasing, development, tax credits, investment structures, risk,
TitleChain evidence, M5Canon authority, UCC rights, credentials, due process,
and source-of-funds provenance.
