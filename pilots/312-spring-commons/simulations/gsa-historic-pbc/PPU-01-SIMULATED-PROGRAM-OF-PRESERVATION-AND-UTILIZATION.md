## 1. Executive program statement

The simulated applicant proposes to preserve and adaptively reuse the property as **312 Spring Commons**, a publicly stewarded historic civic asset. The concept combines public access and historic interpretation with compatible civic, cultural, education, workforce, small-business, occupancy, and resilience uses. Revenue-producing uses would support approved repair, rehabilitation, restoration, maintenance, operations, and public historic-preservation purposes under the controlling statute, deed, approved program, and accounting procedures.

The public entity would retain fee title. Any nonprofit or private operator, developer, tenant, worker, member, service provider, or capital provider would act only under approved instruments and would not acquire an implied fractional deed interest.

This concept remains subject to:

- confirmation that the property is surplus and available for this pathway;
- applicant eligibility and governing authorization;
- NPS suitability and desirability review;
- public and community process;
- title, environmental, condition, preservation, code, utility, and market diligence;
- approved repair, rehabilitation, use, financing, accounting, and maintenance plans; and
- all required federal, state, and local actions.

## 4. Preservation plan

### 4.1 Preservation objective

Preserve the property's historic character, fabric, public significance, and capacity for continued public benefit while introducing only compatible, approved, maintainable uses and systems.

### 4.2 Governing principles

- Follow the Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation and property-specific NPS requirements.
- Retain and repair character-defining features before replacement.
- Prefer reversible, minimally invasive interventions.
- Distinguish stabilization, code/life-safety work, restoration, rehabilitation, maintenance, and new construction.
- Document existing conditions and every approved change.
- Do not permit technology, revenue, branding, or tenant requirements to override preservation obligations.
- Maintain an emergency stabilization and disaster-response plan.
- Fund maintenance and reserves over the full restricted-use period.

## 5. Use and public-benefit plan

### 5.1 Public-purpose framework

The property would be evaluated as a civic platform rather than solely as rentable space. Candidate uses are organized by public purpose and must survive community, preservation, code, demand, and operating review.

| Candidate use | Intended public benefit | Conditions before inclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historic interpretation and public access | Preserve and explain civic history | NPS approval, accessibility, security, operating funding |
| Civic/cultural convening | Public dialogue, exhibitions, events | Use compatibility, capacity, life safety, neighborhood review |
| Education and workforce | Training, apprenticeships, research | Partners, curricula, worker protections, funding, outcomes |
| Small-business/cooperative services | Affordable support, workspace, procurement access | Eligibility, demand, transparent allocation, operating model |
| Compatible occupancy | Stable revenue and active use | Lease authority, preservation compatibility, market validation |
| Food systems | Food access, education, productive use | Food safety, water/energy analysis, demand, operations |
| Energy/water resilience | Reduce costs and support critical services | Historic/structural/utility feasibility and permits |
| Compute/communications | Local digital services and flexible revenue | Security/privacy, energy hierarchy, thermal/noise/load feasibility |
| Repair/circularity | Reuse, skills, reduced waste | Environmental controls, provenance, market and operator capacity |

No candidate use is approved, contracted, or guaranteed.

### 5.2 Community process

The final use plan must be based on a documented, accessible, multilingual process that:

- defines affected communities and outreach limitations;
- includes historically excluded residents, workers, local businesses, preservation stakeholders, and public users;
- compensates community expertise where lawful;
- presents alternatives, costs, constraints, tradeoffs, and a no-project option;
- records support, opposition, unresolved concerns, and responses;
- defines reserved community decisions and public-owner decisions;
- establishes conflicts, removal, grievance, appeal, and amendment procedures; and
- does not treat account ownership, event attendance, or token possession as consent.
