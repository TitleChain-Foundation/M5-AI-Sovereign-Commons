### Preliminary public-purpose concept

Subject to public process, professional diligence, preservation requirements, financial feasibility, and all necessary approvals, the applicant would evaluate preservation and reuse of the historic property as a publicly stewarded civic commons. Candidate compatible uses include civic and cultural programming, education and workforce services, community and small-business facilities, compatible occupancy, and carefully evaluated resilience infrastructure.

Fee title would remain with an eligible public entity and be subject to the federal deed, historic-preservation, public-use, reversion, and other controlling restrictions. Any operator, tenant, developer, member, worker, service provider, or capital provider would receive only the rights expressly created by approved legal instruments.

### Preliminary preservation commitment

The applicant would develop a Program of Preservation and Utilization with qualified preservation professionals and would seek NPS approval for the preservation plan, use plan, repair plan, financial plan, and proposed rehabilitative changes. No physical intervention is authorized by this expression.

### Preliminary financial-capacity statement

The project currently contains a preliminary total-capitalization scenario of approximately $200 million–$240 million and an illustrative initial private-project tranche of $25 million. These are planning assumptions only. No binding capital commitment or escrowed funds are represented.

If the pathway proceeds, the applicant would provide independently reviewed capital and operating budgets, funding evidence, reserves, accounting procedures, and perpetual-maintenance capacity appropriate to the approved plan. The $25 million tranche would support rehabilitation and project obligations under definitive documents; it would not be characterized as purchase consideration to GSA under the working no-cost conveyance pathway.
