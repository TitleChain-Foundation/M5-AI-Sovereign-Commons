Public Review No. 1 — What should Congress require of AI-assisted federal permitting?

Foundation recommendation · Draft for public review · Not enacted law

The Senate passed an amended H.R. 2347 on November 19, 2026. The Senate text would establish federal electronic permitting standards, automate some screening and case management, support AI-assisted review of earlier decisions and public comments, and develop a shared federal portal with vendor-neutral interoperability and a decentralized, cross-network digital infrastructure. The bill is not law merely because the Senate passed it.

The question for the House is not whether agencies should modernize. They should. The question is what evidence Congress will require before an automated system can influence a public decision.

Five questions every permit should answer

  1. Who applied and who has authority?
  2. What legal standards apply?
  3. What tools, agents and calculations were used?
  4. What costs, jobs, energy demand and environmental effects were promised?
  5. What happened afterward?

We propose open method records, independently tested credentials and calculations, human legal accountability, a public project-impact index and the ability to challenge errors. A verified applicant should not need to submit the same trustworthy evidence repeatedly. But a checklist cannot waive environmental law, public participation, or Tribal rights.

A job promise is not a job created. A forecast is not a verified outcome. A faster permit is not necessarily a better permit. A secure AI system is not necessarily an authorized or correct decision-maker.

Implementation and review path

  • Phase 2A — legislative evidence (OPEN): verify amendment boundaries and dispositions; clause-level redline for §§1102–2114; current-law retrieval; submit proposed §209(h)–(i) to legislative counsel.
  • Phase 2B — standards and conformance (DRAFT COMPLETE, TESTS OPEN): open machine-readable schema, legal-gate taxonomy, method certificates, delegation receipts; two-agency/two-vendor prototype with revocation, model-change and error-correction tests; fail-closed unauthorized calls; exportable receipts; accessibility, privacy and independent audit review.
  • Phase 3 — evidence ledger: sourced federal contract and IT-investment records with award identifiers and oversight findings; no blanket characterizations.
  • Phase 4 — scientific model (NOT RUN): verified historical cohorts before any scenario is published; sensitivity, confidence and counterfactual limitations; scientific referee review pending.
  • Phase 5 — publication: staged public review with source links, change log, evidence tiers and human editorial/legal sign-off.

Status: legislative counsel review PENDING · scientific referee PENDING · privacy/security assessment PENDING. These labels are preserved so readers can see exactly what has and has not been verified.

Statutory language implementing these recommendations is published as the House discussion draft (proposed §209(h)–(i)), with the underlying analysis in the review and evidence record.
