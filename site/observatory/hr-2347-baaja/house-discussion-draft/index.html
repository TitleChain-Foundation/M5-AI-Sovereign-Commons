Proposed House statutory language — discussion draft

Foundation discussion draft for legislative counsel review · Proposed additions to NEPA §209 (§1121 of the Senate text) · Not introduced · Not enacted law

This draft proposes two new subsections, §209(h) and §209(i), coordinated with the Senate-passed text's existing §209(e)–(g). It is offered for House consideration of the Senate-amended H.R. 2347, and separately as two companion discussion drafts that first-term House members are invited to consider introducing as standalone bills. No sponsor, introduction, or committee action is established.

Proposed new §209(h) — Verifiable automated permitting and scientific-method accountability

(1) Definitions. An automated participant means a software agent, AI model, rule engine, orchestration service or other automated system that prepares, screens, classifies, recommends, transmits or executes a material action in an environmental review or authorization. A material action means an action reasonably capable of affecting legal eligibility, required review, public participation, permit conditions, timing, or the administrative record. A method certificate means a portable, verifiable record identifying the provenance, version, scope, equations or computational rules, inputs, validation status and limitations of an analytical method; it is not a certificate of government approval unless expressly issued or recognized under applicable law.

(2) Responsible authority. Each material automated action shall be attributable to a responsible agency, identified authorized official, and authenticated executing system. Use of automation or a contractor shall not transfer or excuse statutory responsibility. No final legal determination may be issued solely by an automated participant unless expressly permitted by applicable law and accompanied by any legally required official authorization.

(3) Credentials and delegated scope. The Chairman shall establish vendor-neutral, portable requirements for verification of agency and applicant legal identity, professional qualifications where legally required, automated participant identity, approved function, delegated authority, revocation, and interagency exchange. Credentials shall be purpose-limited and shall not establish rights beyond the issuer's lawful authority. Ownership, custody, operation and delegated control of computational resources shall be recorded as distinct facts where material.

(4) Method registry and scientific conformance. Each material quantitative model used to support a legal or comparative finding shall be identified by a versioned, exportable method record specifying the applicable legal/scientific standard, formulas or reproducible algorithm specification, units, input lineage, uncertainty, limitations, independent validation and review status. Agencies shall accept equivalent conforming implementations and shall not require a particular proprietary product, blockchain, model or credential vendor. Protected code and security-sensitive details may be reviewed under lawful restricted-access procedures without denying independent validation.

(5) Evidence reuse and expedited processing. Agencies shall recognize previously verified evidence to the extent lawful, current, applicable to the specific project and subject to revocation or material change. Automated checks may reduce duplicative filing, triage complete submissions, and recommend administrative routing. No expedited pathway shall dispense with mandatory findings, consultation, public notice, environmental protections, or rights of administrative or judicial review.

(6) Decision receipts. For each material automated action, the agency shall preserve an exportable, tamper-evident record of initiating authority, actor identity, input and source versions, model and rule versions, material tool calls and handoffs, legal criteria, results, exceptions, responsible human approvals and corrections. Sensitive records shall be access-controlled under existing law; public summaries shall not reveal protected personal, Tribal, security or confidential commercial information.

(7) Independent testing and challenge. Before operational deployment and after material changes, agencies shall test cross-vendor and interagency interoperability, failure modes, legal-authority boundaries, accessibility, reproducibility, and security. Agencies shall maintain procedures for correction, human review, and preservation of existing administrative and judicial remedies. AI-supported comment categorization shall not suppress, substitute for, or alter required public participation.

(8) No automated waiver of rights. Neither automated scoring nor credential recognition shall displace Tribal sovereignty, treaty or trust responsibilities, state law within its jurisdiction, environmental standards, consultation duties, or an agency's nondelegable obligations.

Proposed new §209(i) — Public permitting impact index and outcome verification

(1) Open reference framework. The Chairman, in consultation with relevant agencies, states, Tribes and the public, shall publish an open, versioned, machine-readable framework for project impact reporting. The framework shall separate legal thresholds from nonbinding comparative scores and specify units, geographic scope, baseline years, uncertainty and data provenance.

(2) Standard indicators. For covered projects, where relevant and lawful, the framework shall distinguish projected and verified direct, indirect, temporary and permanent employment; public subsidies and tax expenditures; energy and water demand; infrastructure and remediation liabilities; environmental effects; housing and community impacts; public revenue; permitting cost and duration; and realized benefits over time.

(3) Historical comparisons. Comparisons shall use documented cohorts of similar projects and identify selection criteria, inflation adjustment, regional conditions, omitted observations, and uncertainty. Applicant claims shall never be labeled independently verified outcomes.

(4) Public-facing record. The portal shall provide accessible plain-language project summaries, applicable legal criteria, method versions, source links, permits and conditions, public commitments, confidence labels and subsequent verified outcomes, subject to lawful disclosure restrictions.

Companion-bill pathway

Each proposed subsection is drafted to stand alone. The Foundation invites first-term House members and their legislative counsel to consider introducing them as separate measures: a verifiable automated permitting and scientific-method accountability act (§209(h) text) and a public permitting impact index and outcome verification act (§209(i) text). Counsel review of numbering, constitutional, appropriations, APA, NEPA, Tribal and procurement questions is pending; no sponsor or introduction is established.

Coordinated with the Senate text's existing proposed §209(e)–(g); see the review and evidence record and recommendations.
