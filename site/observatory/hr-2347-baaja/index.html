Pinned provision · verified in Senate EAS text

Division C, §3001 — Survivor tax relief

Division C is titled “Survivor Justice Tax Prevention Act.” Section 3001 covers the exclusion from gross income of nonpunitive damages received on account of sexual acts or sexual contact. It appears on printed/PDF pages 483–486.

Open the exact preserved Senate-passed PDF at page 483 · Official GovInfo PDF

The section includes the proposed exclusion, burden-of-proof provisions, effective-date rules, treatment of medical records or observable injuries, and public-awareness provisions. Effective dates are tied to enactment; this is a summary of proposed Senate text, not current tax guidance.

Exact wording is provided in the pinned source PDF, not reconstructed in this summary.

Foundation work on this legislation

The Foundation has published its own review and proposals for H.R. 2347 / BAAJA. Every item below is Foundation work product — clearly separated from the official congressional record above, and none of it is introduced or enacted law.

1 · House discussion draft — proposed NEPA §209(h) and §209(i)

Complete proposed statutory language for the House: verifiable automated permitting with scientific-method accountability (§209(h)) and a public permitting impact index with outcome verification (§209(i)), coordinated with the Senate text's existing §209(e)–(g). Drafted for legislative counsel review; prepared so the House can act on the Senate-amended vehicle and return it for Senate agreement and presentment to the President. Not introduced.

2 · BAAJA review and evidence record

First-pass comparison of the November 10 substitute (SA 6883, preserved Congressional Record extract) against the November 19 Senate-passed text — 83 sections aligned by automated screening — plus a nine-row issue crosswalk of the Senate text against existing federal law. Both source PDFs are preserved with SHA-256 integrity hashes.

3 · Recommendations — Public Review No. 1

What Congress should require of AI-assisted federal permitting: five questions every permit should answer, open method records, human legal accountability, a public project-impact index — and the staged implementation and review path with its pending counsel and referee checkpoints shown honestly.

4 · Two companion discussion drafts for new House sponsors

Each proposed subsection is drafted to stand alone, and the Foundation invites first-term House members to consider introducing them as separate measures: a verifiable automated permitting and scientific-method accountability act (§209(h) text) and a public permitting impact index and outcome verification act (§209(i) text). No sponsor or introduction is established.

Related Foundation proposal

The Sovereign Compute Access Act (Draft v2) — the Foundation/ICSN model bill the Foundation seeks to advance for congressional consideration in 2027 — addresses the same accountability foundations (verifiable agent authority, vendor neutrality, state-primary administration) across all computing, beyond permitting.
