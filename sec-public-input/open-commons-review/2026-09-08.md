# People's Public Trust — Open Commons Review

**Status:** Approved public-interest orientation

**Inaugural review period:** September 1–8, 2026

**Approval recorded:** September 8, 2026

**Reviewer attribution:** TitleChain Foundation repository-owner review

**Official source:** [SEC File No. S7-2026-30](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/public-comments/s7-2026-30)

## Bottom line

The first week of comments does not show agreement on one technology or one
implementation timetable. It does show a common problem: modern securities
records need a clearly accountable operator, an explicit authoritative record,
reliable reconciliation, enforceable correction, successor continuity, and a
way for people to understand and recover their rights.

From the People's Public Trust open-commons viewpoint, modernization should not
replace broker or database opacity with ledger, platform, or agent opacity.
Open standards should make authority, restrictions, evidence, correction, and
portability easier to inspect while leaving legal responsibility with the
accountable human and regulated entity.

## Six takeaways

### 1. The authoritative record must be explicit

Zayn, Vertalo, Conan Mak, Hunter Reed, and TitleChain Foundation approach the
problem differently, but each raises the integrity or reconciliation of the
record. A token movement, wallet balance, broker display, or ledger event should
not be mistaken for legal ownership state unless the responsible recordkeeper
has defined that relationship and can reconcile it.

**Open-commons view:** every implementation should declare which record is
authoritative, who may change it, how conflicts are resolved, and what evidence
proves the resulting state.

### 2. Control of a record is not the same as control of a network

Vertalo asks the SEC to define exclusive control by the transfer agent's sole
authority over the record state rather than by ownership of the underlying
network. Zayn asks for role-sensitive resilience tests. TitleChain Foundation
proposes implementation-neutral interoperability without displacing the
registered transfer agent's books and responsibilities.

**Open-commons view:** use outcome-based tests for authority, correction,
availability, migration, and independent access. Do not require one vendor or
ledger merely because its infrastructure is easier to describe.

### 3. Correction, recovery, and succession are public-interest requirements

Hunter Reed calls for aged record differences and omissions to be reconciled
within a strict implementation period. Conan Mak supports rapid successor
delivery and continuous token-to-master-file reconciliation. Zayn and Vertalo
focus on continuity through network failure, migration, and transfer-agent
replacement.

**Open-commons view:** a credible system must support correction without silent
rewriting, preserve the history and authority for each change, and export a
complete successor package before a provider fails or a relationship ends.

### 4. Automation cannot become its own source of authority

Vertalo asks the SEC to address who is answerable when software acts on records.
TitleChain Foundation proposes credential-bound participant identifiers and
pre-execution gates for accountable principal, current role, jurisdiction,
policy, approval, revocation, and evidence.

**Open-commons view:** an AI model, wallet, key, API credential, or software
agent may execute only authority already established by an accountable human or
legal entity. Every consequential automated action should produce an
attributable authorization receipt.

### 5. Transparency must work for ordinary people

Steven Hartwick asks for assets to be held in the individual's name rather than
remaining under broker control and raises surveillance concerns. Lasagna Kay
Dixon describes difficulty determining whether records or investments relate to
her and difficulty reaching institutions. Anthony Gonzales expresses a broader
fairness and access concern without proposing a specific transfer-agent rule.

**Open-commons view:** transparency is not only a regulator-facing data
requirement. People need understandable records, reliable contact and recovery
paths, and the ability to verify what they own or control. That access should be
purpose-limited so transparency does not become surveillance.

### 6. The duty can remain common even when transition timing differs

Hunter Reed proposes a strict one-year deadline for all legacy reconciliation.
Vertalo proposes the same eventual duties with a 12-month transition for larger
agents and 24 months for agents reporting fewer than 1,000 transfer items,
together with activity-based proportionality.

**Open-commons view:** proportional implementation may be appropriate when
supported by evidence, but it should not create a permanent lower standard for
authoritative records, safeguards, correction, or successor continuity.

## What the People's Public Trust should test next

1. A machine-readable authoritative-record declaration and conflict rule.
2. Continuous reconciliation between token, wallet, broker, and master-file states.
3. Correction, legal-hold, revocation, and court-order evidence without erased history.
4. Transfer-agent replacement with a complete, verifiable successor package.
5. Network halt, reorganization, upgrade-key capture, and provider disappearance.
6. Credential-bound human and entity authority before wallet or agent execution.
7. Consumer-facing identity, contact, recovery, and ownership-verification pathways.
8. A proportional transition model that preserves one eventual public duty.

## Review boundary

This edition is independent public-interest analysis. It is not an SEC position,
does not treat comment frequency as a vote, and does not imply that the
Commission adopted or endorsed any comment. The official filings control.

Approval of this weekly orientation does not change the observatory's
source-level review count: one detailed comment summary is approved and seven
remain pending detailed human review.
