## Bottom line

The refreshed record strengthens the first review's central conclusion: modern
securities infrastructure needs an accountable regulated operator, explicit
records of authority, continuous reconciliation, governed correction, and
portable successor evidence. The newer filings add a second requirement that
is just as important: systems must preserve what the security represents, which
source supports each material fact, which record controls each legal object,
and what changed under whose authority. Moving a token successfully does not by
itself prove that ownership, restrictions, an underlying right, or settlement
obligations changed lawfully.

The record also makes implementation economics part of the public-interest
analysis. Commenters disagree on deadlines and exemptions, but the newer
transfer-agent submissions provide concrete reasons to test compliance costs,
small-agent transition periods, low-volume performance metrics, and
transaction-level documentation requirements against actual operating volume.
From the People's Public Trust open-commons viewpoint, proportional transition
may reduce concentration risk, but it must still lead to one common duty for
accurate records, enforceable restrictions, recovery, cybersecurity, and
successor continuity.

This is a Foundation public-interest orientation, not an SEC finding or a vote
count. Five filings in this review window and the previously omitted September
7 LedgerLab filing remain pending source-level human review in the automated
observatory. The official filings control.

## What changed in the record

### 1. Provenance must begin at issuance

Gene Deyev asks for a dated, sourced, versioned issuance record that distinguishes
issuer assertions, third-party review, and authoritative evidence. Monica
Pagano likewise argues that a financial instrument must remain linked to its
underlying asset or right, supporting evidence, restrictions, and lifecycle
state.

**Open-commons view:** each instrument should have a persistent identity and a
machine-readable evidence map showing what was represented at issuance, where
each fact came from, and how corrections preserve prior states.

### 2. One master file does not make every other record interchangeable

Pagano distinguishes the master securityholder file from records governing
beneficial positions, property title, security interests, contracts, custody,
and settlement. Deyev similarly separates the holder register from the facts
that establish what was issued.

**Open-commons view:** require explicit links among relevant authoritative
records without pretending that a token, wallet, master file, or property
registry can silently replace the others.

### 3. Control should attach to accountable authority, not network ownership

Equity Stock Transfer and BlockAgent, LedgerLab, and earlier commenters support
technology-neutral use of distributed ledgers while retaining one accountable
recordkeeping transfer agent. Their proposals focus on the ability to authorize,
correct, reverse, restrict, reconstruct, and migrate the record rather than on
ownership of the underlying public or private network.

**Open-commons view:** test whether the accountable operator can exercise and
prove lawful control over record state, including under network failure,
software change, key compromise, and successor migration.

### 4. Correction duties need both urgency and durable evidence

Teddy Peppers asks the Commission to define stock-split dividends as covered
items and require rapid remediation of legacy differences. Equity Stock
Transfer and BlockAgent support 15-day successor delivery without withholding
records over fee disputes, while preserving records of the departing agent's
own performance.

**Open-commons view:** correction should be time-bounded, attributable, and
non-destructive. A corrected balance without preserved authority and history is
not sufficient evidence.

### 5. Compliance design must be tested against actual scale

Equity Stock Transfer reports approximately 11,400 legend-removal requests in
2025, compared with assumptions it says would imply roughly three requests per
agent per year. The filing also proposes transition periods, a low-volume floor
for percentage tests, and program-level determinations with transaction-level
verification. LedgerLab describes practical costs and regulatory barriers in a
hybrid tokenized-security pilot. Deyev asks whether standardized issuance
records could lower repeated fixed costs for issuers, agents, and examiners.

**Open-commons view:** use disclosed operational data to test cost estimates and
anti-concentration effects. Proportional implementation should not become a
permanent exemption from record integrity or investor protection.

### 6. Modernization still requires ordinary investor protection

Cici W. supports stronger recordkeeping, cybersecurity, business continuity,
and clear transfer-agent responsibility. The newer technical filings also
preserve privacy by linking controlled or pseudonymous references to verified
offchain identity rather than publishing personal data onchain.

**Open-commons view:** modernization succeeds only when people can identify the
accountable institution, understand the relevant record, obtain correction or
recovery, and rely on safeguards without exposing unnecessary personal data.

## What the People's Public Trust should test next

1. A dated, sourced, versioned issuance record linked to the instrument.
2. A dependency map identifying the authoritative record for each legal object.
3. Wallet-to-holder and token-to-instrument mappings that preserve privacy.
4. Public-network control based on accountable authority, correction, and migration.
5. Stock-split-dividend processing and legacy-difference remediation evidence.
6. Successor delivery that cannot be withheld while preserving prior-agent records.
7. Legend-removal controls tested at reported real-world transaction volumes.
8. Small-agent transition and low-volume metrics that avoid forced consolidation.
9. Cybersecurity, network halt, chain split, reconstruction, and provider-loss tests.
10. Automated-action records that identify the human or entity delegation behind execution.

## Bottom line

The expanded comment record strengthens the case for technology-neutral
modernization, but it also shows that moving a security record onchain does not
by itself make the underlying right, issuance facts, restrictions, authority,
or correction process verifiable. The public-interest requirement is now more
specific: identify the authoritative record for each legal object, link those
records without treating them as interchangeable, preserve sourced and dated
issuance evidence, reconcile onchain and offchain states, and keep an
accountable transfer agent able to correct, recover, migrate, and explain the
record.

The newer filings also make implementation economics part of the trust model.
Uniform duties should not become permanent exemptions, but compliance dates,
low-volume performance tests, audit costs, and high-volume legend-removal
workflows need evidence-based design so modernization does not force smaller
agents or issuers into avoidable consolidation. From the People's Public Trust
open-commons viewpoint, the standard should be common while implementation is
measurable, proportionate, portable, and independently testable.

This is a repository-owner orientation based on the official filings. It is not
an SEC finding, does not treat filing frequency as a vote, and does not convert
any filing's automated topic signals into an approved detailed summary.

## What changed in this review period

### Issuance evidence must travel with the instrument

Gene Deyev asks for a dated, sourced, versioned issuance record that distinguishes
issuer assertions, third-party review, and authoritative evidence. Monica
Elizabeth Pagano similarly argues that technical interoperability is not legal
or economic interoperability and that linked records must preserve the
instrument, rights, restrictions, dependencies, authority, and prior states.

**Open-commons view:** a position should be traceable not only to a holder and
quantity, but also to the evidence establishing what was issued and the rights
it represents.

### Exclusive control concerns the record, not ownership of the network

Equity Stock Transfer and BlockAgent support public-network use when no change
becomes authoritative without the transfer agent's action or revocable
authorization, the agent can correct or reverse entries, and tested migration
controls prevent network or program changes from taking control of the record.
This reinforces earlier comments from Vertalo and LedgerLab.

**Open-commons view:** test authority, correction, reconstruction, and migration.
Do not equate use of shared infrastructure with surrender of the legally
responsible record.

### Linked records must remain reconstructable

The new filings distinguish the master securityholder file, control book,
transfer journal, issuance evidence, holder identity, beneficial positions,
underlying property records, restrictions, and ledger events. They repeatedly
call for reconciliation and durable reconstruction without requiring a
proprietary network or a duplicate shadow register.

**Open-commons view:** require a declared relationship among records, documented
exceptions when states diverge, and evidence sufficient to reconstruct both the
state and the authority for each material change.

### Correction deadlines and implementation burdens remain contested

Teddy Peppers urges a 30-to-60-day cleanup period for legacy differences and
explicit treatment of stock-split dividends. Equity Stock Transfer and
BlockAgent recommend phased 12-to-18-month implementation, activity-based relief
for the smallest agents, and de minimis denominators for low-volume performance
tests. They also provide operational data challenging the proposal's estimated
legend-removal volume and cost.

**Open-commons view:** preserve one eventual public duty, test proposed deadlines
against real operational data, and prevent proportionality from becoming either
a permanent lower standard or a consolidation mechanism.

### Modernization support is broad but not a substitute for issue-level review

Cici W. supports stronger recordkeeping, cybersecurity, business continuity,
and clear transfer-agent responsibility. The more detailed filings support
modernization while proposing materially different rules for issuance evidence,
ledger control, restrictive legends, contact information, incident reporting,
and transition timing.

**Open-commons view:** general support for modernization cannot be treated as
agreement on any particular implementation.

## Updated conformance agenda

1. Declare the authoritative record for each relevant legal object and the rule
   for resolving conflicts among linked records.
2. Bind each issued position to dated, sourced, versioned issuance evidence.
3. Reconcile onchain positions, holder records, control-book supply, transfer
   journals, restrictions, and offchain dependencies without silent overwrite.
4. Demonstrate correction, freeze, recovery, court-order response, and migration
   under the accountable transfer agent's authority.
5. Reconstruct a historical state without depending on one network, explorer,
   vendor, or indexing service.
6. Test public-network control through revocable authority and operational
   capability rather than infrastructure ownership.
7. Measure transition periods, low-volume tests, audit costs, and legend-removal
   controls against actual small-agent and high-volume workflows.
8. Keep automated actions attributable to documented human or legal-entity
   delegation and produce evidence of each consequential decision.
